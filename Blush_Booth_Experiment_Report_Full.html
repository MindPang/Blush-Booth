Blush Booth: face detection, landmark and parser experiments

15 short-run experiments, selection of one model per task, full training of the three winners on LaPa, integration of the fully trained models into the app, and a single final evaluation on the held-out test split. Model choices were made on the validation split only. Revised 23 Nov 2026.

2. Data and protocol

Data. LaPa: 18,168 train, 2,000 val and 2,000 test photos with 106 landmarks and 11 parsing classes. Detection scenes are photos placed on 640x480 canvases, plus empty scenes built from 100 face-free background photos.

Short runs. Every experiment uses the same 2,000 train images (fixed list, seed 0) and the same 500 val images. All CNNs use the same optimiser and schedule: AdamW (weight decay 1e-4), OneCycle learning rate, batch 16, lr 1e-3, 4 epochs. One seed per experiment.

Hardware. All training and every per-model time in sections 1 and 3 to 5 (ms/frame, ms/crop): NVIDIA RTX 3060 12 GB PC. The test-set evaluation (section 7) and all app timings (section 8): NVIDIA RTX 3050 6 GB Laptop GPU. Times from the two machines are never compared with each other.

Selection rule (fixed before the runs). Detection: highest recall with false alarms at most 3% and at most 15 ms per frame. Parser: highest mIoU with lips IoU within 0.02 of the best and at most 30 ms per crop. Landmark: lowest NME with at most 15 ms per crop.

Full training. The winner's own script is rerun on all 18,168 train images with the same optimiser and batch size, for 8 epochs (parser) or 20 epochs (landmark, detection).

Metrics. Detection: recall (IoU above 0.5 and score above the threshold), mean IoU, false-alarm rate on empty scenes. Landmarks: NME as a percentage of the outer-eye-corner distance over all 106 points; failure = NME above 10%. Parser: mean IoU over 11 classes (background included).

4. Models selected

Detection: D4 YOLOv8n at threshold 0.4 (recall 0.954, false alarms 1.8%, 6.6 ms). Parser: P5 U-Net + ResNet18 (mIoU 0.788, 5.5 ms). Landmark: L5 heatmap model (NME 6.0%, 4.3 ms); times measured on the RTX 3060 PC. All three met their speed limits by a wide margin, so speed did not change any decision. The short-run selection is saved in best_models.json; the fully trained weights are listed in model/best_models.json.

6. Using the fully trained models in the app

After full training, the app and the browser server were still loading the short-run weights: both defaulted to best_models.json, while full_train.py writes the fully trained weights to model/best_models.json. This has been fixed:

  • facekit/pipeline.py: new default_config() returns model/best_models.json when it exists, otherwise best_models.json.
  • makeup_app.py and ui/server.py: --config now defaults to that choice. At start-up both print the model file and the stage of each model, e.g. models: model/best_models.json (detector full, landmark full, parser full). --config best_models.json still selects the short-run models.
  • experiments/test_once.py: the one-time test-split evaluation now uses the fully trained models too.
  • model/best_models.json: its val_metrics still showed the short-run scores; they now show the full-training scores above, and the short-run scores are kept under short_run_val_metrics.
Checks. Photo mode loads all three full models and applies makeup correctly to a face photo, and correctly reports "no face" on a face-free background. The browser server loads the full models and returns a made-up frame with a face detected (POST /api/frame). The project test suite passes (33 tests).

7. Test-set results (held-out, evaluated once)

8. Live-camera speed: short-run vs fully trained weights on the same GPU

Both weight sets were timed on the same machine (NVIDIA RTX 3050 6 GB Laptop GPU, PyTorch 2.12, CUDA 13.0) with the same script, which calls the same functions as the webcam loop: 40 LaPa photos letterboxed to 640x480, default look 'soft pink' (all layers on, including hair), GPU-synchronised timings, 400 frames with tracking (each photo held for 10 frames). The runs alternated short, full, short, full; each value is the mean of the two runs per weight set (the two runs agreed within 10%). A face was found in all 40 photos in every run.

Full training does not change the size of the networks, and on the same GPU the two weight sets run at the same speed (differences of about 1 ms per stage are within run-to-run noise). The networks take only a small part of the frame; most of it goes to CPU work around them: makeup blending, colour conversions, the skin-smoothing filter and the second parser pass for hair. A smooth live feed needs about 25 fps (under 40 ms per frame), so the app code, not the models, is what needs optimising. The earlier report's speed table (short-run weights, 100 ms per frame) was measured on the RTX 3060 PC and is not comparable with these numbers.

9. Limitations

  • Model selection used the validation split; the test split was evaluated once, at the end, and was not used for any decision. Detection test scenes are synthetic, and their empty scenes come from only 15 background photos.
  • One random seed per experiment, so small differences (a few hundredths of mIoU or recall) are within run-to-run noise.
  • The short runs use 2,000 training images and 4 epochs; approaches that need more training were disadvantaged, especially the from-scratch CNNs. Only the winners were fully trained.
  • The detector (COCO) and parser (ImageNet) start from pretrained weights; the landmark winner trained from scratch. If a project rule requires training from random weights, the best from-scratch alternatives that pass the selection rules are D3 CenterNet (short-run recall 0.630 at 0.4% false alarms) and P4 U-Net (short-run mIoU 0.370).
  • Detection scenes are synthetic (face photo pasted on a canvas); real webcam frames may behave differently.
  • Two machines were used: per-model times in sections 1 and 3 to 5 come from the RTX 3060 training PC; app timings in section 8 come from the RTX 3050 laptop. Section 8 was measured on photos, not a live camera, and its 40 photos are training images (fine for timing, not used for any accuracy figure).
